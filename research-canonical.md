## 1 · What the live SERPs say about the market GSLEY is entering

Three patterns emerged across the searches that anchor every persona below.

**1.1 The Lagos digital agency market is crowded and undifferentiated.** Listicles for *"best digital marketing agency Lagos / Nigeria"* surface the same handful of incumbents repeatedly — Wild Fusion (decade-plus, large enterprise brands), ID Africa, Big Field Digital, Ziza, Oxgital, Kong, BetterOffService, SA Media. Almost all position on service categories (SEO, PPC, social, web design) rather than methodology or commercial outcomes. Wild Fusion is the senior incumbent; Oxgital is the only one in early 2026 explicitly leading with *"AI-powered marketing"* — and even that reads as a service add-on, not a worldview. **GSLEY's "productised offers, owned methodology, AI-native delivery" framing is genuinely uncrowded in this market.**

**1.2 Healthcare marketing has a single visible specialist in Lagos.** SA Media positions explicitly as *"Top Healthcare Digital Marketing Agency in Lagos, Nigeria."* That is the direct comparator any institutional buyer Googling for hospital marketing help will encounter first. Their register is service-led (SEO, paid, social) — a sample to differentiate against, not imitate.

**1.3 The global "AI-native consultancy" register is settling around three motifs.** *Pipeline predictability.* *Owned operating systems* — Brandtelling's "Story Operating System™," Kalungi's "GTM operating system." *The AI Efficiency Paradox* — GROW Powered's framing for the now-recognised pattern that more AI tooling produces flat pipeline without strategic judgement. GSLEY's "documented archetypes — never opinions" lives inside this register but with an institutional and African lens that none of the global comparators carry.

## 2 · Persona 01 — The Institutional Buyer

**Profile.** Hospital admin, foundation director, government parastatal lead, subsidiary of a sovereign-investment vehicle. Reports to a board or trustees. Owns commercial outcomes for an organisation where reputation is the operating asset. Lagos, Abuja, Nairobi, Accra primarily; sometimes Geneva or DC for the multilateral version.

**Real titles in market.** Director of Commercial Services. Head of Business Development. Chief of Staff to the Managing Director. Director of External Affairs. Head of Partnerships and Programmes. Director of Communications. Director of Strategy and Innovation.

### Top 5 fears
1. The next AI pilot fails publicly — board, donor, or regulator notices before internal mitigation is ready.
2. A reputational incident — a search result, a social-media moment, an undisclosed conflict — surfaces and the institution cannot respond at the speed news now moves.
3. Marketing budget is spent without producing institutional weight. Spend goes up; perception does not.
4. A consultant signs the NDA, produces a deck, and disappears, leaving the internal team holding half a system.
5. The new operating model cannot be run by the team after the engagement ends.

### Top 5 desired outcomes
1. A documented framework that survives a board change.
2. AI deployment with explicit governance — bias review, data-residency posture, model lifecycle ownership.
3. Predictable institutional partnerships (banks, payers, donors, accreditation bodies) that produce co-funded programmes rather than single transactions.
4. Earned media that reaches policy and donor audiences, not only consumer audiences.
5. A succession-ready commercial operation.

### Phrases this persona Googles
- *healthcare marketing agency Lagos*
- *digital transformation hospitals Africa*
- *AI governance framework health institutions*
- *institutional reputation management Nigeria*
- *corporate communications consultancy Lagos*
- *fractional CMO healthcare Nigeria*

### LLM-search prompts this persona will type
Constructed from observed conversational-search patterns. Longer than Google queries; carry context.

- *"What does a credible AI-adoption programme for a 200-bed Nigerian hospital actually look like? Who has done it well?"*
- *"How do I structure a multi-stakeholder partnership between a private hospital, a bank, and a donor in West Africa?"*
- *"My foundation's board is asking for an AI strategy. What should I send them before the next quarterly meeting?"*
- *"What are the most common reasons AI pilots fail in African healthcare institutions?"*
- *"Who in Lagos does institutional brand and reputation management at the level a CEO would trust?"*

### Discovery-call objections
- *"We've worked with consultants before. They produced a PDF and disappeared."*
- *"Our data cannot leave the country."*
- *"The board has approved an AI strategy in principle but has not approved a budget."*
- *"We need to see this work somewhere comparable before we sign."*
- *"Our internal team is at capacity — can your team run it, or do we?"*

### Proof artefacts that move this persona
- A founder bio that signals chartered credentials (MCIPR, executive programmes from named business schools) and visible board-level engagement.
- A documented methodology with the brand's own name — never borrowed jargon.
- Work cited at the *category* of institution (Tier-1 financial institution, international medical group, sovereign-backed vehicle) without breaching confidentiality. *(GSLEY's posture per founder direction.)*
- A clearly stated AI governance and data-handling stance on About / Methodology.
- A diagnostic that produces a board-ready PDF, not a marketing-newsletter scorecard.

### Copy implication for GSLEY
The Founder Note drafted in Turn 2 is calibrated for this persona. The diagnostic tool route for this persona is the **AI Adoption Assessment — Organisation**, not the Growth Diagnostic; the CTA architecture should let this persona reach that tool in two clicks from the homepage. The About page needs a dedicated AI-governance and data-handling stance — a paragraph, not a footnote.

## 3 · Persona 02 — The Enterprise Growth Leader

**Profile.** CMO, CRO, VP / Head of Growth at a $10M–$500M B2B business. Frequently venture-backed or PE-backed. Pipeline accountability runs to the CEO and to investors. CMO tenure in this segment is short by reputation; every one of these people is working in a partial-honeymoon, partial-survival window.

**Real titles in market.** Chief Marketing Officer. Chief Revenue Officer. Head of Growth. Head of Demand. VP, Revenue Operations. VP, Go-to-Market.

### Top 5 fears
1. Pipeline is unpredictable enough that the next board meeting becomes adversarial.
2. The AI tooling stack grew faster than the strategic judgement behind it — what GROW Powered has named the AI Efficiency Paradox: more output, flat pipeline.
3. Attribution will not survive due diligence in the next fundraise.
4. Marketing leadership turnover creates a known valuation discount at exit.
5. Hiring a fractional and then realising the operator is selling thought-leadership content, not building infrastructure.

### Top 5 desired outcomes
1. Pipeline architecture that is documented and transferable, not held in one person's head.
2. CAC payback shortened by a specific stage-level intervention, not a generic "growth strategy."
3. Investor-grade dashboards.
4. A go-to-market system the next CMO inherits intact.
5. Category positioning that survives a fundraise.

### Phrases this persona Googles
- *fractional CMO B2B SaaS*
- *pipeline predictability framework*
- *GTM operating system* — Kalungi has eroded this query; GSLEY needs a different proper noun
- *marketing attribution due diligence*
- *AI-first marketing engine*
- *B2B demand generation system*

### LLM-search prompts this persona will type
- *"Compare fractional CMO firms that have built AI-native go-to-market engines for PE-backed B2B SaaS."*
- *"What does a defensible pipeline architecture look like that I can show a Series B investor?"*
- *"Why is my AI tooling stack not producing pipeline?"*
- *"What's the difference between a fractional CMO and a productised growth studio?"*
- *"Who is doing serious AI-search optimisation work for B2B SaaS in 2026?"*

### Discovery-call objections
- *"We've hired three agencies that all said the same thing."*
- *"Our board wants attribution before they fund the next channel."*
- *"Our existing fractional says they don't do paid acquisition."*
- *"We need someone who can stand in a board meeting, not just write content."*
- *"Send me a teardown of our funnel before we book another call."*

### Proof artefacts that move this persona
- A named, documented framework — not "our approach."
- A teardown-style sample (anonymised) demonstrating diagnostic depth.
- A position paper on AI-search readiness in 2026 written as an operator's view, not a generic blog post.
- A clear engagement structure on each service page — given GSLEY's price-on-request decision, the engagement structure must do the disambiguation work that pricing usually does (sprint scope, deliverables, time-to-value, what is *not* in scope).
- Visible founder credibility on LinkedIn.

### Copy implication for GSLEY
The strategic-tier service pages — Demand Generation, B2B Sales Enablement, Marketing Automation — are the primary surface for this persona. Each must lead with the named methodology (the Archetype block) and a teardown-style proof block. The matched tools are the **Marketing Maturity Scorecard** (diagnostic surface) and the **Revenue Leakage Calculator** (financial frame). Insights articles for this persona need to be written as operator views, not thought leadership — closer in register to First Round Review than to a corporate blog.

## 4 · Persona 03 — The Ambitious Founder / CEO of a B2B or B2C Scale-up

**Profile.** Founder or CEO of a Nigerian or West African B2B / B2C company that has cleared product-market fit and is trying to compound out of the bootstrap phase into category leadership. Revenue band roughly ₦500M–₦15B annual. They are simultaneously head of sales, head of brand, and — increasingly — head of AI strategy, because no one else on the team is asking the question. They Google in English with Nigerian context.

**Real titles in market.** Founder / CEO. Co-founder & COO. Founder & MD. Country General Manager (for the West African subsidiary of an international scale-up).

### Top 5 fears
1. The brand looks generic when stood next to a global competitor in the same category. Category creation in Nigeria carries a double burden — educate the customer *and* justify the category — that local founders are acutely conscious of.
2. The sales cycle is too long for the cash position.
3. The team cannot ship a website, a campaign, or a product launch without the founder rewriting it.
4. AI tooling has been bought but is not producing measurable productivity gains.
5. The founder personally has to be every PR spokesperson, every BD lead, every content creator — there is no scalable narrative system.

### Top 5 desired outcomes
1. A brand system that operates without the founder in the room.
2. Predictable B2B pipeline from named accounts.
3. A category-creation narrative the founder can repeat across boardrooms, podcasts, and investor decks unchanged.
4. AI-augmented operations that release the founder's calendar.
5. A website that doubles as a hiring magnet and a sales asset.

### Phrases this persona Googles
- *best digital marketing agency Lagos Nigeria* — verified, dominated by listicle SERPs
- *B2B lead generation Nigeria*
- *how to build a category in Nigerian market*
- *website design for B2B Lagos*
- *AI automation small business Nigeria*
- *Google Ads for B2B Lagos*

### LLM-search prompts this persona will type
- *"I run a Lagos-based B2B fintech with ₦2B in revenue. What kind of marketing studio should I hire — a Nigerian agency or a global fractional CMO?"*
- *"How do I build category leadership in a Nigerian B2B market where the buyer doesn't know the category exists?"*
- *"What's the most efficient stack for a Nigerian B2B founder to run paid acquisition and AI-automated lifecycle in 2026?"*
- *"Who in West Africa is doing thought-leadership development for CEOs at a board level?"*
- *"What does a productised growth sprint look like and how does it differ from a retainer?"*

### Discovery-call objections
- *"I've already paid two agencies who gave me Canva designs."*
- *"I need someone senior — not a junior account manager dressed up as one."*
- *"Show me what you'd do in the first 30 days."*
- *"We tried Google Ads before. It didn't work."*
- *"We can't afford a retainer right now — what's your sprint?"*

### Proof artefacts that move this persona
- A 30 / 60 / 90 sprint plan visible on the service page.
- A diagnostic with a sample scorecard — the existing site already does this on page 6 of the PDF; protect it.
- A founder bio with chartered credentials and senior-board exposure. *(Already drafted.)*
- Concrete service deliverables, not vague promises.
- A website that loads fast on a Tecno or Infinix — the Anti-Slop Charter and the Lighthouse mobile gate are doing this work, but it must be tested on real devices, not only on simulated ones.

### Copy implication for GSLEY
This is the primary commercial target for the front-line services per the Turn 2 direction. The Website Design page, the Google Ads page, the Email Marketing page, and the Social Media Marketing page must be calibrated specifically to this persona's vocabulary and engagement length. Matched tools: **Growth Diagnostic** (already brand-named in the existing site) and **Automation Opportunity Finder**.

## 7 · Strategic findings to carry into Turn 4 and beyond

**7.1 The uncrowded seam.** No incumbent in the Nigerian agency market positions on documented methodology, productised offers, and AI-native delivery together. GSLEY's editorial restraint and named-archetype frame is the strongest positioning lever available, and it is reinforced — not weakened — by the front-line services Chukwudi has prioritised. The Sprints can carry the editorial voice; they do not have to abandon it for conventional service-led copy.

**7.2 The institutional buyer is GSLEY's high-margin tier; the founder/CEO is the volume tier.** Persona 01 will produce fewer, larger engagements; Persona 03 will produce more, smaller ones. The site needs to serve both without diluting either. The two-tier value-ladder design from the existing site (Tier 02 / 03 Sprints for Persona 03; Tier 03 / 04 Retainers and Bespoke for Persona 01) is the right structural answer. Confirmed in this research; no change recommended.

**7.3 AI-search is the differentiator no incumbent owns.** Wild Fusion, ID Africa, Big Field, and the other senior Lagos agencies are largely silent on AI-search readiness. Oxgital is the only one with an AI mention and it is "AI-powered marketing tools," not LLM-citation strategy. GSLEY's `llms.txt`, structured FAQ blocks, named methodologies, and citation-ready content are an unclaimed moat **if shipped at launch**.

**7.4 The CMO-tenure dynamic.** Persona 02 is a buyer with short time horizons. Sprint-shaped offers convert better than retainer-shaped offers for this persona because the sprint completes inside the buyer's likely tenure. GSLEY's value ladder already handles this; the service pages need to reinforce it explicitly in the engagement-tier section.
